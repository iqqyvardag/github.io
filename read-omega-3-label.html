How to read an omega-3 supplement label

A practical method for separating total oil, EPA, DHA, serving size, source and formulation—without turning label arithmetic into a quality score.

Published by TriOrigin LabsEditorially reviewedLast reviewed: 16 July 2026

The largest number on an omega-3 bottle is often the total oil, not the amount of EPA and DHA. A careful reading starts with the Supplement Facts panel and the serving size.

The five-step label check

  1. Find the serving size. Determine how many capsules, teaspoons or gummies make one serving.
  2. Find EPA and DHA separately. These amounts are more informative than “fish oil 1,000 mg” alone.
  3. Add EPA and DHA. This gives the clearly stated combined amount per serving.
  4. Convert to per capsule if useful. Divide by the number of capsules in the serving.
  5. Read source and form carefully. Fish, krill and algae describe sources; triglyceride, re-esterified triglyceride, ethyl ester and phospholipid describe chemical forms.

A fictional example

Serving size2 softgels
Fish oil1,000 mg
EPA360 mg
DHA240 mg
EPA + DHA600 mg per serving / 300 mg per softgel

In this example, “1,000 mg fish oil” is not the same as 1,000 mg EPA+DHA. The combined stated EPA and DHA are 600 mg.

What concentration percentage can and cannot tell you

Dividing stated EPA+DHA by total stated oil gives a simple label concentration. In the example, 600 ÷ 1,000 = 60%.

That percentage does not establish oxidation status, contaminant levels, capsule integrity, identity, manufacturing quality, absorption in a specific person or superiority in a health outcome.

Be cautious with front-label claims

Terms such as “high potency,” “maximum strength” or “premium” are not substitutes for the Supplement Facts amounts. A claim about molecular form is also not proof of a clinical advantage unless the relevant source, dose and outcome have been studied.
