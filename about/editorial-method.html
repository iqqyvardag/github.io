Editorial method

How we develop evidence-led content.

How TriOrigin defines questions, selects sources, interprets evidence, handles review and discloses limitations.

TriOrigin LabsLast assessed 17 July 20266 min read

On this page

TriOrigin’s method is designed to make the reasoning behind important content visible. It is not presented as a substitute for formal guideline development or a full systematic review unless a page explicitly documents that process.

1. Define the scope before drafting

Health questions are framed using the population, intervention or exposure, comparator and outcome. We distinguish foods, ordinary supplements and prescription medicines because findings from one context cannot automatically be transferred to another.

2. Use a source hierarchy

Core claims are traced to authoritative guidance, regulatory documents, systematic syntheses and original studies. Secondary explanations may help with language, but they do not carry the central evidentiary burden.

3. Separate results from interpretation

Statistical significance is not treated as automatic clinical importance. Surrogate outcomes, subgroup findings, post hoc analyses, conflicts and indirectness are identified when materially relevant.

4. Match review to risk

Foundational educational content receives editorial fact-checking. Clinical, safety or treatment-adjacent conclusions require qualified specialist review before they should be described as externally reviewed. Until named review is completed, TriOrigin states the current review status plainly.

5. Make uncertainty visible

Evidence is not forced into a positive or negative slogan. We state what remains unknown, who was not represented and what readers should not infer.

6. Separate evidence from commerce

TriOrigin Labs is operated by TriOrigin LLC and may later sell products. Editorial conclusions must not be arranged merely to justify a future formula. When commercial content is introduced, it should be visibly separated from editorial content.

7. Update and correct

Important pages show a review date. Material changes in authoritative guidance, safety information or underlying evidence trigger reassessment. Confirmed material errors are corrected with an appropriate public note.
