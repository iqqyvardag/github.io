Evidence standards

Confidence belongs to a defined conclusion.

How TriOrigin uses confidence labels and keeps conclusions tied to a defined population, intervention, comparator and outcome.

TriOrigin LabsLast assessed 17 July 20266 min read

On this page

Confidence domainsNot a quality badgeIndividual studies

A confidence label does not rate omega-3 as a whole. It applies only to a precisely stated conclusion and may change when the population, dose, formulation or outcome changes.

High

Robust and generally consistent evidence for the defined conclusion.

Moderate

Useful evidence exists, but limitations or inconsistency materially reduce certainty.

Limited

Evidence is sparse, inconsistent, indirect or methodologically constrained.

Preliminary

Early or indirect evidence that cannot support a firm conclusion.

What shapes confidence

  • Risk of bias in the underlying studies.
  • Consistency of the direction and size of findings.
  • Directness to the stated population, intervention and outcome.
  • Precision and statistical uncertainty.
  • Publication bias and selective reporting.
  • Agreement with authoritative synthesis and guidance, when applicable.

Confidence is not a product-quality badge

A high-confidence conclusion about prescription omega-3 lowering triglycerides does not certify a retail product, establish the best formulation or prove a different clinical outcome.

One study does not receive a confidence rating

Individual studies contribute to a synthesis. A confidence rating belongs to the combined evidence for a defined conclusion, not to a single paper presented in isolation.
